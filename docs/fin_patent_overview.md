# Sentiment Analysis Overview

## End-to-end flow diagram
```mermaid
graph TD
    A[Input ticker symbol] --> B[DataFetcher aggregates news
    from Alpha Vantage, Finnhub, Yahoo Finance, Reddit]
    B --> C[Deduplicate, filter for ticker relevance,
    backfill with deterministic blurbs]
    C --> D[Summarize & weight stock-specific vs. market news]
    D --> E[Historical sentiment loader
    (optional JSON or API feed)]
    E --> F[Go optimizer recomputes weights,
    baseline, and confidence signals]
    F --> G[Prompt builder creates ordered
    weighted bullet list]
    G --> H[Preferred LLM provider
    (Ollama → DeepSeek → OpenAI fallback)]
    H --> I[Structured response parser
    (score, confidence, reasoning)]
    I --> J[Apply Go adjustments
    + enrich summary]
    J --> K[Final sentiment, metadata, and
    recommendations to downstream consumers]
```

## Narrative walkthrough
1. **Ticker intake** – A client requests sentiment for a symbol. The analyzer normalizes the symbol and determines whether it maps to equities or crypto to adjust the tone of the later prompt.
2. **News collection and filtering** – `DataFetcher` fans out to Alpha Vantage, Yahoo Finance, Finnhub, and Reddit, merges the results, deduplicates headlines, and aggressively filters for the requested ticker. When the set is sparse, deterministic fallback blurbs keep the pipeline alive.
3. **Summarization and base weighting** – Each surviving article contributes:
   - a base weight (ticker-focused stories are boosted),
   - a compact summary generated by `_summarize_text`, and
   - provenance metadata such as source and publication timestamp.
   Articles are sorted by recency so the eventual prompt reflects the latest developments first.
4. **Historical enrichment (optional)** – If `Config.HISTORICAL_SENTIMENT_PATH` is configured or the caller passes `historical_sentiment`, the analyzer loads prior sentiment decisions, realized returns, confidence, volume, source, and timestamps for the same ticker.
5. **Go-based optimization** – The combined article list and historical rows flow to `go/cmd/sentiment_optimizer`. The helper recalibrates article weights, computes a baseline sentiment shift, nudges confidence based on historical variance, and emits diagnostics/notes describing why adjustments were made.
6. **Prompt construction** – `_build_weighted_prompt` turns the optimized set into numbered bullets of the form `Weight 1.5 (Reuters) — Headline
   Summary: …`. This keeps the prompt short while preserving the relative influence of each source.
7. **LLM inference** – The prompt is submitted to the preferred provider (Ollama locally, falling back to DeepSeek or OpenAI). The model is instructed to return JSON containing `sentiment_score`, `confidence`, and `summary`.
8. **Post-processing** – The analyzer validates ranges, applies the Go optimizer’s baseline and confidence nudges, appends the optimizer’s notes to the textual summary, and packages rich `analysis_metadata` (prompt stats, counts, optimizer diagnostics) for downstream UIs.
9. **Recommendation surface** – The final sentiment bundle feeds trading heuristics and dashboards that combine the score with strategy backtests, alerts, or portfolio suggestions.

## Mathematical formulation
Let \(T\) be the requested ticker. After filtering we have \(n\) articles \(A = \{a_1, \dots, a_n\}\). Each article \(a_i\) carries:
- base weight \(w_i > 0\),
- source label \(s_i\),
- publication time \(t_i\), and
- textual summary \(x_i\).

### 1. Prompt-ready weights
For each article the Go helper computes
\[
\tilde{w}_i = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0.2, \infty)}\left(w_i \cdot f_{\text{source}}(s_i) \cdot f_{\text{recency}}(t_i)\right).
\]
The source factor is derived from historical alignment:
\[
f_{\text{source}}(s) = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0.4, 1.6]}\left(1 + 0.6 \cdot \hat{q}_s\right),\quad
\hat{q}_s = \frac{\sum_{j \in H_s} \alpha_j\, (\sigma_j \cdot r_j)}{\sum_{j \in H_s} \alpha_j},
\]
where \(H_s\) indexes historical rows sharing source \(s\), \(\sigma_j\) is the recorded sentiment, \(r_j\) the realized return, and \(\alpha_j\) the confidence/volume/time-decayed weight chosen by the Go helper.

Recency uses the article age \(\Delta t_i\) in hours:
\[
f_{\text{recency}}(t_i) =
\begin{cases}
1.18, & \Delta t_i \le 12,\\
1.08, & 12 < \Delta t_i \le 48,\\
0.82, & \Delta t_i \ge 120,\\
1.00, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]

### 2. Historical drift signals
Define weighted accuracy totals across all historical rows:
\[
A_{\text{tot}} = \sum_j \alpha_j (\sigma_j r_j), \qquad
W_{\text{tot}} = \sum_j \alpha_j.
\]
If \(W_{\text{tot}} > 0\) the optimizer emits a baseline shift
\[
\beta = \operatorname{clamp}_{[-0.3,\,0.3]}\left(\frac{A_{\text{tot}}}{W_{\text{tot}}}\right),
\]
and a variance diagnostic
\[
\nu = \max\left\{0, \frac{\sum_j \alpha_j (\sigma_j r_j)^2}{W_{\text{tot}}} - \left(\frac{A_{\text{tot}}}{W_{\text{tot}}}\right)^2\right\}.
\]
The variance governs a confidence increment \(\delta_c\):
\[
\delta_c =
\begin{cases}
0.08, & \nu < 0.01,\\
0.04, & 0.01 \le \nu < 0.04,\\
-0.05, & \nu > 0.20,\\
0, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]

### 3. LLM inference and final aggregation
The prompt \(P\) fed to the LLM is the ordered list of \((\tilde{w}_i, s_i, x_i)\). The model returns \(s_{\text{llm}} \in [-1,1]\) and \(c_{\text{llm}} \in [0,1]\). Post-processing applies the historical drift:
\[
s_{\text{final}} = \operatorname{clamp}_{[-1,1]}(s_{\text{llm}} + \beta), \qquad
c_{\text{final}} = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}(c_{\text{llm}} + \delta_c).
\]
The textual summary is augmented with up to two optimizer notes (e.g., “Reuters historically aligned with price action”).

### 4. Recommendation surface
Downstream consumers combine \((s_{\text{final}}, c_{\text{final}})\) with portfolio constraints, technical indicators, or alerting rules to generate trade suggestions. Metadata passed alongside the score includes counts of ticker-specific vs. market articles, prompt token footprint, and the optimizer diagnostics \(\{\hat{q}_s\}\).

## Worked example
Consider a request for ticker **MSFT**:

1. **Articles gathered**
   - *Article 1:* Reuters headline, base weight \(w_1 = 1.2\), published 4 hours ago.
   - *Article 2:* Reddit discussion, base weight \(w_2 = 0.8\), published 48 hours ago.

2. **Historical records** (same ticker):

| Source  | Sentiment \(\sigma\) | Return \(r\) | Confidence | Volume | Age |
|---------|-----------------------|--------------|------------|--------|-----|
| Reuters | 0.4                   | 0.3          | 0.8        | 2.0M   | 6 h |
| CNBC    | -0.3                  | -0.1         | 0.6        | 0.5M   | 30 h|
| Reddit  | 0.6                   | -0.2         | 0.4        | 0.1M   | 240 h|

The Go helper calculates \(\alpha_j\) for each row, yielding weights of \(1.25\), \(0.65\), and \(0.2665\) respectively. Weighted accuracy totals:
\[
A_{\text{tot}} = 0.15 + 0.0195 - 0.032 = 0.1375,\qquad
W_{\text{tot}} = 1.25 + 0.65 + 0.2665 = 2.1665.
\]
Hence the baseline shift is
\[
\beta = \operatorname{clamp}_{[-0.3, 0.3]}(0.1375 / 2.1665) \approx 0.064.
\]
The variance \(\nu \approx 0.0063\) triggers the high-confidence boost \(\delta_c = 0.08\).

Per-source alignment scores are:
- Reuters: \(0.15 / 1.25 = +0.12\)
- CNBC: \(0.0195 / 0.65 = +0.03\)
- Reddit: \(-0.032 / 0.2665 = -0.12\)

These drive the article-specific weight adjustments:
\[
\tilde{w}_1 = 1.2 \times (1 + 0.6 \cdot 0.12) \times 1.18 \approx 1.52,\qquad
\tilde{w}_2 = 0.8 \times (1 + 0.6 \cdot (-0.12)) \times 1.08 \approx 0.80.
\]

3. **Prompt & LLM outcome** – The analyzer builds two bullets ordered by recency and submits them to the configured LLM. Suppose the model returns `{"sentiment_score": 0.35, "confidence": 0.72, ...}`.

4. **Post-processing** – Applying the optimizer adjustments:
\[
s_{\text{final}} = \operatorname{clamp}_{[-1,1]}(0.35 + 0.064) = 0.414,\qquad
c_{\text{final}} = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}(0.72 + 0.08) = 0.80.
\]
The summary string gains a historical note: “Historical context: Reuters historically aligned with price action (+0.12).”

5. **Recommendation** – Downstream systems observe moderately bullish sentiment with elevated confidence, possibly surfacing a “watch for upside continuation” alert or ranking MSFT higher in a sentiment-driven screener.

## Operational considerations
- If no articles are available, the analyzer falls back to `analyze_price_based_sentiment`, relying on price momentum and technical cues.
- When AI providers fail, a neutral sentiment stub is returned so dependent services remain resilient.
- Optimizer diagnostics (`historical_alignment`, `source:*`, `notes`) are exposed in `analysis_metadata['go_optimizer']` to help analysts audit the recommendation.
